What changed from the spreadsheet

  1. Weighted returns. The sheet used AVERAGE() inside each type, so a 12.5% fund counted as much as a 45% fund. Now each fund counts by its size.
  2. Ups and downs. The sheet grew every year by the same %. Here 2,000 random futures include crashes (with “fat tails”, so big drops happen about as often as in real markets). Crypto is modelled with 65–80% yearly swings.
  3. Inflation. €30,000 in 2058 buys about what €16,000 buys today at 2% inflation. Everything is shown in today’s money unless you switch the table.
  4. Tax on profit only. The sheet took 30% of every withdrawal on top. In real life only the profit part of what you sell is taxed. The tool tracks what you paid in, so it knows the profit share each year.
  5. Fees. Each fund’s yearly fee (TER) and a buying cost are subtracted.
  6. Timing. Money you add during a year grows for about half a year, not a full year.
  7. “Total interest” fix. The sheet’s total interest (end balance minus money put in) forgot the money taken out and the tax. The tiles above count it correctly.
  8. Fixed links. The Moderate and Aggressive tabs used the Safe capital cell ($A$3). Here each mix uses the real balance.
